Two theses and the method underneath them. Both are strongest where they report a negative result, so that is where these summaries start.

MSc thesis · UCL · in write-up, submission Sep 2026

Latent Dynamics of Prediction-Market Price Paths

Supervised by Prof. Philip Treleaven, University College London

A prediction-market price is a terminally-absorbed process: it is driven to a $0/$1 outcome at an approximately known deadline. The thesis asks what governs how, and how fast, such a market reprices — and treats that as a latent-structure learning problem, because no observable field records it.

The intuition to beat was that repricing should sharpen into the deadline as the answer approaches. It does the opposite. Intensity freezes, and that freeze sits beneath a much larger, faster clustering process — a two-scale picture the thesis reaches by pre-registered test rather than by argument.

Reversal — the conjecture was wrong

Repricing intensity freezes rather than sharpens as a market approaches the end of its activity: β_τ = −0.131, CI [−0.188, −0.074], with 8 of 9 markets frozen at the first cohort. Re-anchoring at the settlement record makes it stronger. The direction held from n=9 through n=318 under frozen specifications.

Evidence — pre-registered, with recovery and identifiability gates that had to pass before any empirical number was read; both directions and the null admissible in advance. Caveat — on the trade-tape re-run the freeze survives as a median-market property but the pooled-rate gate fails; that robustness slice is graded partial.

Null — the primary pre-registered test failed

The thesis's own primary hypothesis was that a consensus term would improve prediction beyond a calendar term. It did not: −1.1 nats, CI [−394.6, +409.1] at n=318, with 46% of markets positive. The thesis calls this "a genuine equipoise, not an underpowered near-miss", and states the consequence against its own interest — the licensed claim becomes mechanism-existence, not mechanism-primacy.

Evidence — pre-registered, leave-one-market-out, market-clustered bootstrap, carried from n=105 to n=318 with zero re-specification.

Reversal — a post-hoc competitor beat my own headline

A pre-registered comparison found a self-exciting model predicting repricing far better than the two-state latent-state model: +48,970 nats, CI [+35,238, +67,016], zero drop-one flips. After the pre-registration closed I tested the flank I had disclosed — and a three-state competitor reverses that ordering by 37,868 nats, CI [+30,156, +46,662]. A simulation control rules out a parameter-count artifact.

So the claim was narrowed rather than defended: the result is scoped to the tested two-state model, not to the model class. One-step-ahead count likelihood does not separate the two accounts.

Evidence — the winning arm pre-registered and recovery-gated; the competitor exploratory, post-freeze and not pre-registered. Both numbers are published together or neither.

Negative — my own model class lost its benchmark

A pre-registered neural benchmark put the interpretable models on trial, and they lost: an LSTM beat the champion by +15,600 nats, CI [+10,675, +21,640], ahead on 51/51 held-out test markets. The interpretable ladder still covers 90.8% of the Poisson-to-LSTM gap, CI [88.2, 92.7], using 3 to 6 parameters.

The direction of the bound is the unflattering one, and is reported that way: the learning curve is still rising, so +15,600 is a lower bound on the neural advantage and 90.8% is an upper bound on the interpretable share.

Evidence — pre-registered, falsification-tested in both directions, test set evaluated exactly once. Disclosed deviation — a single frozen split at n=206, not the leave-one-market-out standard used elsewhere. Model-selection validation only: no costs, no fills, no trading claim.

A fifth result is the one I would defend hardest in an interview, because it is the least flattering: the best-fitting model in the ladder fails its own absolute goodness-of-fit test. A time-rescaling check rejects it on most markets. It is the best-performing tested approximation, not a generatively adequate description — and the residual clustering it misses is the same headroom the neural benchmark exploits.

BSc thesis · Leiden (LIACS) · 2025

Self-supervised models for human activity recognition

Fine-tuning General Time Series and Accelerometer-Specific Self-Supervised Models for Human Activity Recognition. Six architectures benchmarked head-to-head on HARTH v1.2, 12 activity classes, under leave-one-subject-out cross-validation.

The load-bearing finding is negative

A Friedman test failed to reject the null at α=0.05: there is no statistically significant difference in macro F1 among all six models on this dataset and protocol. The Nemenyi post-hoc was therefore not run — there was no difference to decompose. The thesis states that the observed spread "could reasonably be attributed to random chance."

The practical finding

SelfPAB — the state-of-the-art model the thesis was built around — cost about 7 hours for the full cross-validation, against 1.7 hours for SimCLR and 23 minutes for XGBoost. It finished fourth, statistically tied with everything else. A 23-minute gradient-boosting baseline was indistinguishable from a 7-hour transformer.

Recorded honestly rather than claimed: SelfPAB scored 0.860 here against 0.813 in its original paper on the same dataset, attributed to setup variation and not claimed as an improvement. Known persistent limitation: class imbalance.

Method

How I keep myself honest

The reason to trust any number above is the protocol that produced it, so here is the protocol — including where it falls short.

  • Specifications committed before outcome statistics. The MSc work carries 14 pre-registered components, each indexed as a spec-commit-then-results-commit pair. The one amendment is disclosed as pre-outcome under its own hash: the originally specified gating statistic failed simulated false-positive control at 35% against a 5% bar, so an already-registered alternative was promoted before any empirical statistic was computed.
  • Recovery gates before results. Several tests had to demonstrate, on simulated data with a known truth, that they could recover the right answer — and that they would return the null when the null was true — before any real number was read.
  • The null declared admissible in advance. "No effect" and "no identifiable structure at this sample size" were written into specs as acceptable outcomes, which is what makes reporting them afterwards meaningful rather than merely disappointing.
  • Clustered bootstraps with the right unit. Market-clustered resampling throughout, so the effective sample is the number of markets rather than the number of events.
  • Held-out and walk-forward evaluation. Leave-one-market-out as the house standard, chronological splits where time ordering matters, and any deviation from that standard disclosed at the point it is used.
  • Multiple-comparison control. Benjamini–Hochberg across every test family, applied before a result is called.

Where the protocol falls short

Pre-registration did not prevent two of my own metrics from turning out to be artifacts of their measurement design — one apparent lead tracked the length of its own lookback window exactly, and one rising hazard was an artifact of a minimum-length floor. Both were diagnosed, dissected, and the verdicts rebuilt on artifact-free re-analyses.

Beyond that: the research repository is private, so the commit ordering is not reader-verifiable; no library versions or containers are pinned anywhere; and not every figure comes from a pre-registered component. The cohorts are nested rather than independent, and the corpus is six weeks of one venue's liquid, actively-repriced head.
