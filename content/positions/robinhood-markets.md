## Thesis

*Written 2026-08-23.*

**Robinhood is where a generation is learning to hold assets, and the money is now following the
accounts.** That second part is what turned this from a story into a position. Owning the default
financial app of people who are early in their earning lives is a very long-dated asset, provided
they actually bring their money — and they are.

**The quarter.** Net revenue of $1.31bn, up 32%. Net income of $573m, up 48%. Adjusted EBITDA of
$741m at a 57% margin, and that margin is being carried while the company spends heavily on
marketing and new products. This is a profitable business, not a growth story that hopes to become
one.

**Platform assets reached $369bn, up 32%, with $21.7bn of net deposits in the quarter — about a 28%
annualised rate.** This is the number that matters most to me. User counts can be vanity; net
deposits are people moving real money onto the platform, and at that rate the asset base compounds
without needing a single new customer. As those balances grow with their owners' careers, the
revenue per account grows with them.

**And it keeps shipping.** Agentic Trading, Robinhood Earn, Robinhood Chain, and Rothera — a
CFTC-licensed exchange and clearinghouse through a joint venture. That last one matters more than it
sounds: owning licensed market infrastructure is a different kind of business from routing orders to
somebody else's. This is a company still expanding the surface it can charge on.

## What I am watching

*I am bullish, and these are the things I would need to see to change that.*

**How much of the profit is the market rather than the business.** Transaction revenue grew 44% and
is the largest driver, and transaction revenue is the most cyclical line a broker has. It rises with
enthusiasm and falls with it. Net interest revenue grew 9% by comparison — the steadier half is
growing far more slowly than the exciting half.

**The reported EPS is flattered.** Diluted EPS of $0.62 was up 48%, but $0.14 of it relates to a fund
deconsolidation. That is a real event and it is not the operating business, so the underlying
progress is smaller than the headline.

**What a drawdown does to this.** I have not owned this through a bad market. The thesis says the
deposits keep arriving because the accounts are habitual rather than speculative. A sustained
downturn is the test of that sentence, and it has not happened yet.

**Regulatory exposure.** A retail broker expanding into crypto, prediction markets and its own
clearing infrastructure is widening the number of regulators with an opinion about it.

## What would make me wrong

- Net deposits slowing sharply, which would say the habit is thinner than I think.
- Transaction revenue falling in a drawdown with no other line taking the weight.
- Margin compressing as the newer products scale, rather than holding at these levels.
- A regulatory outcome that changes the economics of order flow or of the new venues.
