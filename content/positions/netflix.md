## Thesis

*Written 2026-08-23.*

**Netflix won streaming, and I got to buy the winner during a de-rating.** The competitive question
that hung over this company for a decade has been answered: the rivals that were going to spend their
way in have mostly retreated to licensing their content back to it. What is left is the largest
subscription entertainment business in the world, with the pricing power that comes from being the
one service people will not cancel.

**The stock came down a long way, and that is the opportunity.** Shares are roughly **41% below their
peak and down about 26% so far this year**, and the multiple has compressed from a historical 35-40
times forward earnings to somewhere around **20-24 times**. That is not a distressed business being
marked down. It is a mature, profitable, cash-generating one being repriced from a growth multiple to
something much more ordinary.

**The business itself is doing roughly what it said it would.** Revenue of $12.6bn, up 13%, in line
with the company's own forecast, with the strongest expansion in Latin America and Asia-Pacific —
the markets with the most subscribers still to add.

**And management is buying it too.** Netflix put through a record buyback. A company retiring its own
shares at a compressed multiple is management taking the same side of this trade I am, with better
information than I have.

**What I am actually buying is durability rather than acceleration.** A service people keep, that
raises prices without losing them, that produces cash reliably, bought at a price that no longer
assumes rapid growth. If it merely stays what it is, that works from here.

## What I am watching

*I am bullish, and the honest version of this position starts with why the market disagreed.*

**Growth is decelerating, and the company said so.** Revenue grew 13% and guidance implies Q3 growth
**below 12%**. Revenue also came in fractionally under consensus. That is the whole reason for the
fall — not a scandal, not a competitor, just a slower number and a forecast of a slower one still.
The de-rating is a rational response to that, which means it is not automatically an overreaction.

**A multiple halving is the market changing its mind about what kind of company this is.** Going from
35-40 times to 20-24 times is a re-rating from growth to mature. If that judgement is right, the
cheapness is accurate rather than an opportunity, and the return has to come from earnings rather
than from the multiple recovering.

**Pricing power is the load-bearing assumption.** With subscriber growth slowing, more of the revenue
line has to come from charging existing members more. That works until it does not, and the point at
which it stops working shows up as churn after a price rise rather than as a warning.

**Content spend is the cost of the moat.** The library has to keep being refreshed at enormous
expense simply to keep people where they are. That is the permanent tax on this business.

## What would make me wrong

- Revenue growth falling below 10% with no offsetting margin expansion.
- Churn rising after the next price increase, which would say the pricing power has a limit.
- Content spend rising without engagement or revenue following it.
- Growth in Latin America and Asia-Pacific slowing before those markets are properly monetised.
