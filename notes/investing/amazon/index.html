Thesis

Written 2026-08-21.

The cloud business is accelerating and the retail business is a margin story waiting to happen. AWS grew 36.7% year on year in Q2 2026 at a 37.7% operating margin, and it has now accelerated for five consecutive quarters, from 17% a year earlier.

The retail argument is about automation, and the arithmetic is forgiving. At Amazon's volume a single point of margin is a very large number, so the case does not need heroics. North America already runs at 7.9% operating margin and International at 4.1%, and the company expects to more than double its fleet of robotic arms in 2026. Every point of automation lands on an enormous revenue base.

I do not see this business being disrupted. The moat is physical - the fulfilment network, the logistics, the installed base - and a physical moat does not fall to a better model.

What could take this apart

Free cash flow has gone. Amazon raised 2026 capital expenditure guidance to $220bn from $200bn, and trailing twelve-month free cash flow is an outflow of $7.6bn against an inflow of $18.2bn a year earlier. In a single quarter free cash flow fell from $26bn to $1.2bn. The operating business is performing; the cash is going into the ground.

Part of that increase is a cost I am also being paid by elsewhere. Amazon attributed the raise from $200bn to $220bn partly to higher memory prices - the same memory cycle I hold Micron and SanDisk for. I am long both sides of that trade. It is not a hedge, because the two legs are not the same size and will not turn at the same time, but it is worth knowing that one position's cost is another position's revenue.

Recent reported profit is flattered by something that is not the business. Q2 included a $53bn gain related to Anthropic. That is a real increase in value and it is not operating performance, and any margin comparison that includes it is not like-for-like.

The margin thesis is the slowest-moving part of the case. Retail margins have improved for years and are still single digit. Automation has been arriving for a decade. This is a position to hold expecting gradual improvement, because gradual is what the record shows.

What would make me wrong

  • AWS growth decelerating back toward the high teens while capex stays at this level.
  • Free cash flow staying negative into 2027, which would make the build-out a structural change in the business rather than a phase of it.
  • Retail margins flat for two more years, which would say automation is being competed away in price rather than kept.
  • A serious challenge to the fulfilment network's economics, whether regulatory or from labour costs.
