Thesis

Written 2026-08-21.

Roughly half the planet uses something Meta owns every day, and the ad system attached to it is getting better rather than merely bigger.

That chart is the strongest thing in this thesis. Ad impressions rose 14% and average price per ad rose 12% in Q2 2026 against the same quarter a year earlier. Those two normally trade against each other - more inventory usually means a lower price per unit. Both rising at once says the targeting is improving, and that is where AI is showing up in the accounts even though the company does not break it out.

Revenue was $60.8bn, up 28%, with advertising at $59.4bn, up 27%.

On capex I take the other side of the sell-off. Meta has been short of compute and leasing it. Spending to own that capacity is not a vanity project; it is a company declining to rent its own future. Meta has said before that intermediaries - Apple's tracking restrictions in particular - cost it a great deal, and the lesson it drew was to stop depending on anyone else's platform. Owning the compute is that same lesson applied one layer down.

The case against, which I went looking for

The quarter was not as clean as I described it. Diluted earnings per share were $6.18, down 13% year on year, and missed consensus by roughly 14%. Free cash flow was $784m against $8.55bn a year earlier - a fall of about 91% - because capital expenditure of $31.1bn consumed roughly 98% of operating cash flow. Over the last twelve months, capex including finance leases has run at 41% of revenue. The ad engine is strong. The company around it is currently converting almost none of that strength into cash.

I was wrong about the lawsuit, in a way that matters. I described it as a $1.4tn claim about child protection that does not touch much revenue. What is actually happening: a jury found Meta liable in March 2026, one case has produced $942m in awards, and the federal trial with California, Colorado, Kentucky and New Jersey opened in August 2026. The $1.4tn is a theoretical ceiling on civil penalties, and the states have said they are not seeking a specific figure.

The exposure is not the revenue from minors. It is product remedies. The claims are about design - infinite scroll, algorithmic recommendation, the like button. A court-ordered change to those mechanics would not be scoped to teenagers' spending; it would reach the engagement machinery of the whole platform. Twenty-six more states are behind these four.

Impression growth is decelerating. The chart above shows 19% in Q1 against 14% in Q2, with pricing flat at 12%. Both are still positive, which was my point, but the volume half is slowing.

One argument of mine does not belong on this page. I said that historically, buying Meta at these multiples and after these trends has worked out. That is reasoning from the outcome backwards, which is the exact thing this notebook exists to avoid, so I am striking it rather than dressing it up.

What would make me wrong

  • Price per ad flattening while impressions keep decelerating. That combination would say the AI improvement has been harvested and the volume story is over at the same time.
  • A product remedy that reaches the core recommendation system, rather than a fine. A fine is a number; a remedy is a change to the machine.
  • Capex rising again into 2027 without revenue acceleration to match, which would make the compute build an expense rather than an investment.
  • Free cash flow near zero for several more quarters. One quarter of 98% reinvestment is a decision. A year of it is a different company from the one I bought.
